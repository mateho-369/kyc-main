## Non-restorations and impact

* Dormant `middleware/auth-unified`, `security-enhanced`, `api-protection`, SQL-injection helper stacks and `controllers/documentController` are **not all mounted by original server.js**. Finding a function does not establish original application protection. Do not blanket-remount regex filters/alternate auth pipelines: they can conflict with canonical identity, routes and errors. Parameterized ORM usage is not a completed whole-repository SQL-injection audit. Impact: no claim of these modules' full policies or document encryption on the live performer path.
* Fake disabled-Firebase users, predictable fallback credentials, test-key/admin shortcuts, metadata-derived role grants, broad debug/file endpoints, external status overrides and verification-triggered final approval are deliberately not restored. Impact: clients relying on unsafe legacy shortcuts must migrate; they do not get compatibility privilege escalation.
* The signed inbound approval route is disabled rather than stripped of HMAC while still writable. Its original signature/freshness control is real, but the business operation conflicts with local-review authority and lacks the agreed replacement contract. No unsigned replacement is enabled.
* CORS suffix trust was deliberately removed because newly accepted ambient cookies made compromised siblings dangerous. Explicit deployment origins remain supported. Strict automatic-refresh cookies were **actually restored with regression tests**, not merely listed as a loss.
* Legacy refresh/logout CSRF policy, raw proxy trust, permissive CSP, distributed rate/replay enforcement, WS auth, V1 transport, operational secret rotation and real DB/receiver tests remain explicit release gates. They are not silently waived or described as original-equivalent.
